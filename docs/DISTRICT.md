# Kawasan Perjalanan

This additive browser chapter implements the requested direction in three parts: free exploration and missions, a driveable vehicle, and improved scene presentation. It is a compact fictional training district, with original procedural art. It does not attempt GTA VI's scale, assets or visual fidelity.

## Play

Open `district.html`, also linked from the lobby and chapter map. Choose a male or female avatar. Follow five objectives: speak with Pak Farid → collect luggage → board the bus → drive and stop in the terminal parking bay → disembark and speak with Bu Salma. Each completed objective awards 20 points; the completed district awards 100 total, derived from progress so replaying a conversation cannot duplicate rewards. After the mission, walking and bus exploration remain available.

Two optional activities now open after the main district mission. Meet Pak Hasan in the courtyard and accompany him to the shelter (+30 points), then collect Pak Rizal's parcel from the northern market and hand it to Bu Salma (+40 points). Total district reward becomes 170 after all activities. Neither activity gates the existing manasik chapters. The mission-book button shows the main objectives and both activities at any time.

The same book now reads Tawaf, Sa’i and Tahallul checkpoints and offers the next available chapter. The chapter map presents the combined four-part playable journey, while all three chapter pause/completion menus provide a return to the group. Existing chapter saves can continue without replaying the district. This combined view is read-only and does not create a new save format or award chapter completion.

Farid, Salma, Hasan, Rizal and the roaming pilgrims now offer repeatable questions about the journey and the current destination. Choose “Ke mana saya harus pergi?” for directions or “Ceritakan tentang perjalanan ini” for the character's role, then return to the same conversation. Answers follow the main objective, escort waiting/arrival state, carried parcel and chapter prerequisites. Characters acknowledge completed help and do not offer a collected/delivered parcel again. Asking questions does not accept/complete a mission, write narrative flags or grant points. Existing explicit mission buttons still handle those transitions.

Hasan uses an authored crossing path. He pauses while a dialog is open, when the player is driving, when the bus obstructs the path, or when the player moves more than seven metres away; distance waiting clears below six metres. The marker points back to him while he waits. Each reached path node is a checkpoint, so reloading an active escort resumes beside the last reached node. Parcel possession also survives reload. Final rewards require a conversation at the actual destination, not just reaching a marker.

The bus mission has a rounded route line on the minimap, steering cues, off-route guidance and a parking/braking instruction. These are navigation aids; the player still controls the bus and its original arrival condition. For meeting Hasan and market/delivery walks, a small visibility graph finds routes around buildings and planters. Static graph edges are cached, endpoint links are sampled against the existing collision solids, and the route updates at most every 300 ms. This is a walking guide through the authored district, not traffic-aware autonomous driving.

- Desktop: WASD/arrows walk; Shift walks faster; drag to orbit horizontally/vertically; E interacts or boards/exits; Space brakes while driving; Escape opens the menu.
- Mobile landscape: left joystick moves or supplies throttle/steering, drag the canvas to orbit, buttons handle fast walking, interaction, camera reset and braking. Braking supports a separate held pointer. Portrait, menus, tab hiding and focus loss clear movement input.
- The bus is an arcade kinematic bicycle model, with acceleration, drag, braking, limited reverse speed, and steering that reverses in reverse gear. Its four wheels roll from actual collision-resolved travel and its front wheels follow the steering angle. It has no suspension, engine audio or vehicle damage. Exit requires low speed and a free landing point.

## Implementation

`district.ts` coordinates input, dialogue, ordered missions, animated NPCs, HUD and saves. `districtModel.ts` contains the mission state machine, validation, collision and vehicle movement. `districtWorld.ts` builds the scene; `district.css` provides responsive HUD/dialogs. Vite includes the new HTML alongside all original entries.

`districtDialogue.ts` builds a small typed dialogue graph from existing validated mission/companion/chapter state. Indonesian display strings use stable IDs. Nodes yield choices with exactly one branch, end, existing command or whitelisted chapter link; there is no expression evaluation, dialogue scripting dependency or remote chat service. A validator checks text/targets, duplicate choice IDs, exits and reachability. Branches update the same open native dialog, keep gameplay paused, reset scroll/focus to the first new choice and preserve Escape-to-close behavior. The dialog describes its visible text for assistive technology and announces changed text politely. This is authored contextual dialogue, without voice acting, facial animation or additional story chapters.

The district reuses Three.js r185, original character geometry/vertex animation, animation clips, graphics quality controls/adaptive render budget, procedural material helpers, ambient sound and the shared third-person camera/input. NPCs follow authored loops and yield near the player; guides face the player. This is a small finite-state population, not a general traffic or conversation simulation. Decorative cars on the distant road are scenery.

The camera uses shoulder framing on foot and a wider chase framing in the bus, with delayed heading follow, vertical orbit, bounded speed-based FOV and building occlusion. Boarding/disembarking blends framing, focus and the shortest yaw arc over 0.65 seconds; orbit pitch is retained. Checkpoint teleports reset the camera immediately. The system reduced-motion preference skips the new mode blend and speed FOV. Menus freeze mode progress. Scene presentation adds warm directional/hemisphere light, environment reflections, fog, textured paving/stone, repeated facade details, plants, furnishings, signs, road markings and a terminal parking area. Static meshes and rigid bus parts are merged by material; each moving wheel retains two merged material batches. Original VAT character instancing and device quality profiles limit render work. This art remains stylized and procedural.

`districtPresentation.ts` keeps visual state separate from mission, input and collision state. The player, patrol NPCs and Hasan crossfade between the existing idle/walk clips over 0.18 seconds. Stride follows distance travelled on foot; driving does not advance the player's walk phase. Pausing freezes the submitted pose, and disembarking restores idle. Patrol heading changes ease toward the authored path without changing path positions. Bus wheel spokes are original procedural geometry; wheels roll backwards in reverse and stop when collision or braking prevents travel. These visual states are transient and add no save fields. Boarding still changes control mode immediately; there is no door-entry or seated-driver character animation.

## Save compatibility

Main mission/character saves use the tenant-scoped `manasik-district-v1` and its `.backup`. Version 1 stores `{version,stage,avatar}`. The last valid previous record becomes a backup; a corrupt primary can recover from that backup; a newer-version primary is never overwritten. Browser storage failure is visible in the HUD. Position is restored to a safe mission checkpoint, with the bus parked beside it; exact street coordinates are not serialized.

Optional activities use their own tenant-scoped `manasik-district-errands-v1` and `.backup`, storing `{version,stage,escortGate}`. This separate record leaves the original district save format intact. It uses the same valid-backup, corrupt-primary recovery, quota-failure and future-version protections. Rewards are derived from activity completion, never accumulated by repeated conversations.

The classic adventure, Tawaf, Sa’i and Tahallul keys are preserved. The terminal reads the existing validated chapter progress to offer available destinations: Tawaf is available, Sa’i requires Tawaf completion, and Tahallul requires Sa’i completion. The new district does not unlock or reset existing chapters. Existing training, account/cloud features, assets and chapter entry points stay accessible. Graphics preferences use the project's existing shared preference.

## Verification

`npm run test:core` includes `tests/district.test.ts`: ordered/idempotent mission completion, save validation/backups/newer versions/storage failure, safe spawns, acceleration/reverse/braking, forward/reverse steering, 30/60/120Hz equivalence, thin-object collision, blocked exits, parking and foot collision. Browser regressions run with `npm run qa:district` against the Vite development server. The harness uses real keyboard and CDP touch input, with read-only development snapshots; it contains no state-mutating game test hooks. Diagnostics are excluded from production builds.

The browser journey covers dialogue, luggage, boarding, steering, braking, parking, disembarking, the terminal guide, completion/reload, old-save isolation and chapter locks. Touch checks cover the female avatar, backup recovery, simultaneous joystick/look, independent finger release, portrait freezing and clean landscape resume. Reports and screenshots are generated under ignored `qa-output/`. Results are recorded in `GAMEDEV-AUDIT.md`.

`tests/districtActivities.test.ts` checks activity ordering/reward idempotence, valid and invalid saves, backup/future-version protection, companion distance hysteresis, vehicle waiting, full path reachability, safe checkpoint spawns, steering/parking cues and collision-free walking routes. `npm run qa:activities` exercises the escort and parcel journeys using real browser controls, including intermediate reloads, waiting and modal pauses, plus mobile parcel recovery, mission-book/portrait handling and touch handover.

`tests/districtPresentation.test.ts` checks crossfade/clip boundaries under rapid starts and stops, exact pause poses, distance-based stride at 30/60/120 Hz, camera mode blending/short yaw arcs/quick reversals/checkpoint cuts, and collision-resolved forward/reverse wheel travel. `npm run qa:presentation` observes real keyboard actions and read-only development snapshots: female walk/idle transitions, paused animation, boarding/exiting camera blends, front-wheel steering, rolling/reverse/parked wheels, checkpoint reset, existing-save isolation and system reduced motion. Screenshots/reports use the ignored `qa-output/district-presentation-*` paths.

`tests/districtDialogue.test.ts` walks every question branch over main/side/waiting/chapter combinations, checks malformed graphs, state read-only behavior, command eligibility, chapter locks, acknowledgements and exactly-once reward reducers. `npm run qa:dialogue` exercises actual keyboard/touch questions, branch/back/close, focus and movement blocking, Farid's mission transition, Hasan's checkpoint handover, Rizal's carried-parcel reply, and Salma's completed-help/tenant/locked-chapter links on mobile landscape. `node tools/district-dialogue-qa.mjs --production` runs the same DOM-based checks against the production preview; it does not require development diagnostics.

Physical Android/iOS performance, thermal behavior and audio listening still need device validation. Chrome software rendering is useful for functional checks and draw-call counts; it does not establish phone FPS. Rendering requires WebGL; failure leaves links to the existing map/classic adventure. No deployment or external asset import is included in this change.
